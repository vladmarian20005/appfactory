The reward

Fifty notches, and it is scored

A full stave is dated and stood in the rack.

A stave is ten gates long. When the fiftieth notch lands, the gates light one after another, a single stroke is cut corner to corner across the whole board, the date is stamped into its end and it stands up in the rack behind the bench. A bare one slides on in its place.

For reps that might be one evening. For glasses of water, about a week. The rack keeps every one, oldest at the back, in the colour you painted its end.

The cut

Count anything, no account

One stave, one thumb, and the number is right.

The chisel rests on the stave's shoulder at the next notch. Press and the blade bites, the V opens, a chip flies, and the blade walks one step along — so your thumb always knows where the next one lands, and you can count without looking down. Every fifth cut strikes the gate closed, and you can hear the gate fill: the tone climbs through four and resets on the fifth.

Took one too many? The wax stick fills the last notch. The number goes back; a paler mark stays on the wood, the way it does on a real tally stick. Nobody minds a wax.

The bench

A stave for each thing you keep

Reps, birds, glasses, cars past the window.

Every counter is a stave on one screen, each with its own pigment painted on the end and its running total burned into the wood. The chisel on each one cuts without opening anything. Set a goal and a chalk line goes on a brass gauge; leave it off and the stave just fills.

Under each stave the carver reads your record back in one sentence — "Thursdays run a third above the rest." — and only ever says something that is already true. There is no streak, no flame, and nothing that resets to zero. A day you did not count is simply a gap in the strip.

Questions

What people ask about counter apps

Is there a banner ad across the bottom?

No. There is no advertising SDK in the app at all, so there is nothing to switch on later. The check every release goes through fails the build if one ever appears.

Do I need an account to count to three?

No. There is no sign-up screen, because there is no server behind this. You open it, lay a stave on the bench, and cut.

Does it work offline?

Completely. Tallies never makes a network connection of any kind. It counts the same in airplane mode, in a basement gym, or at the far end of a field.

Can I count without looking at the screen?

That is what it is built for. The chisel always rests where the next notch will land, each cut is a bite and then a release under your thumb, and the tone resets every fifth, so you can feel and hear a gate close.

What if I tap one too many?

Press the wax stick. The last notch is filled and the number goes back by one. The wax stays visible on the stave as a paler mark, so the record is right and it is honest about how it got there.

Is this a habit tracker? Will it nag me?

No. There are no streaks, no reminders and no notifications. Tallies never asks for a permission. A day you did not count is a gap in the strip, and nothing is said about it.

Can I read back what the week looked like?

Every stave has a strip of recent days under it, a gate for each day cut as deep as that day's count. With Pro, the ledger shows every cut on every stave, by the day, with a total for the week and the month.

What happens if Pro lapses?

Nothing you have cut is taken back down. Every stave, every notch and the whole rack stay on your phone. Three staves and a week of strip stay free for good.

What do you collect about me?

Nothing. No account, no analytics, no tracking. Everything you count is on your phone and nowhere else. The privacy policy says the same thing at greater length.
